## About Me

I am an Assistant Professor at the Inter-University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics (IUCAA) in Pune, India, where I co-lead activities at the Optical-UV-NIR Instrumentation Lab with [Prof. A. N. Ramaprakash](https://www.iucaa.in/en/faculty-research/anr).

I am an observational astronomer and an instrument builder, working at the intersection of astronomical instrumentation and observational astrophysics, with a particular focus on polarimetry, spectroscopy and study of interstellar dust.

Before joining IUCAA in 2026, I held postdoctoral positions at the University of Oxford (UK), the South African Astronomical Observatory (South Africa), and the Foundation for Research and Technology – Hellas (FORTH, Greece). I completed my PhD at IUCAA in 2022, where I worked on the design and construction of wide-field optical polarimeters called WALOP for the PASIPHAE sky survey.

## My Research

Astronomy, at its core, is an experimental science; almost always, new discoveries have been preceded advances in measurement and observational techniques and instruments. My research is driven by the realisation that in order to make advances in astrophysics, the key is the development of advanced telescopes and instruments to facilitate newer or better ways to probe the Universe. Motivated by this, I have been aiming to develop new instruments and observational techniques that enable measurements previously difficult or impossible to achieve.

My approach is to develop new techniques and instruments, test them on the sky, and use them to address exciting scientific questions. This involves working across the full instrument development cycle, from scientific motivation and instrument design to laboratory characterisation, commissioning, observations, and scientific analysis.

A major focus of my research is precision polarimetry and spectroscopy, particularly their applications to understanding interstellar dust, magnetic fields, and the physical processes shaping our Galaxy. More recently, I have gotten involved in developing advanced instrumentation for the direct imaging and characterisation of Earth-like exoplanets, as part of the Planetary Camera and Spectrograph (PCS) programme for the 39-m Extremely Large Telescope (ELT).

You can read more about my work on the [Research]({{ '/research/' | relative_url }}) page.
